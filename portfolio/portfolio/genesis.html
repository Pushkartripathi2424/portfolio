Genesis

Your money foundation, step by step. A guided app that helps young Indian earners make their first big money decisions with confidence.

RoleUX/UI Designer
TypeB.Des final UX assignment
ToolsFigma
OutputPrototype + case study deck

The problem

Young Indians get their first salary with no plan. They say yes to EMIs and impulse buys without seeing the real cost, and most finance apps are built for people who already understand money.

The question I set out to answer: how might we give first-time earners plain-English help at the exact moment they make a money decision?

Process

  1. Research. Looked at how young earners handle salary, EMIs and savings, and pulled out their main pain points.
  2. Defined the core flows: onboarding, a monthly Plan, an EMI Check before committing, an Offer Check, and a "Sleep on it" pause for big purchases.
  3. Built a "ladder" model. Instead of overwhelming users, Genesis shows one next best step at a time: know your numbers, then build a buffer, then get health cover.
  4. Designed the brand and UI: a wallet-and-spark logo, a calm violet palette, and Playfair Display italic accents for a human, friendly tone.
  5. Prototyped and refined the screens in Figma, in both light and dark themes.

Key screens

Plan home shows your next best step (e.g. ₹4,500 saved of a ₹36,000 buffer), quick actions and your progress up the ladder.

EMI result turns a "₹3,500 a month" offer into the truth: ₹3,153 extra over the cash price, plus a slow-month stress test showing the instalment is 70% of income in your lowest month.

Visual system

Soft violet glows and frosted cards keep money conversations calm rather than scary. Orange and red appear only when something needs attention.

Result

A complete clickable Figma prototype in light and dark themes, a brand system and a final case study deck.

What I learned: adding friction in the right place, like "Sleep on it", can be good UX. Sometimes the best design slows people down.
